RESEARCH PROJECT / CIRCULAR OPERATIONS

Many Happy Returns? Forecasting Product Flows for a Circular Wales

Cardiff Business School · PARC Institute of Manufacturing, Logistics and Inventory

Understanding the timing, volume and condition of returning products, and how forecasts can inform circular operations.

Focus: Circular product flowsPerspectives: Reuse · Repair · Recycling · RemanufacturingFormat: Research & impact poster

01 / OVERVIEW

Project overview

This project explores forecasting for the circular economy, where products and materials return after use rather than moving through one-way production and disposal. Reverse flows introduce uncertainty about when products will return, how much will be returned and in what condition.

The research connects these forecasting questions with operational decisions across different circular strategies. Its emphasis is on understanding the processes and defining useful forecasts before selecting or evaluating forecasting methods.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Research objectives

  • Characterise circular return flows and the operational uncertainty they create.
  • Identify the forecasting questions most relevant to return timing, quantity and quality.
  • Connect operational needs to research questions through practice engagement, process and data scoping.
  • Explore how forecasting evidence could support decisions, policy dialogue and value preservation.

03 / FRAMEWORK

Research framework

The poster contrasts linear material flows with circular systems in which returned products and materials re-enter the operational process. It uses four settings to show how the returned object and the resulting decisions differ across circular strategies.

Reuse

Library of Things / Benthyg. Borrowed items are inspected, catalogued and made available for further use.

Repair

Electronics / Sony. Returned products are inspected, repaired and returned to service.

Recycling

End-of-life electronics / The Royal Mint. Returned materials are recovered and can re-enter production.

Remanufacturing

Printers and cartridges / Brother. Returned products are inspected, remanufactured and reintroduced to use.

04 / QUESTIONS

Key forecasting questions

WHEN?Return timing

When will products or materials become available again?

HOW MANY?Return volume

What quantity of products or materials can be expected?

WHAT CONDITION?Return quality

What condition and recovery potential might returns have?

05 / IMPACT

Pathways to impact

The poster outlines a route from partner engagement and operational problem definition to forecasting questions, evidence and potential benefits. These are intended pathways rather than completed impact outcomes.

Operations

Better-informed operational decisions in circular processes.

Across sectors

Transferable insights into the forecasting of return flows.

Policy

Evidence that can inform dialogue on circular economy priorities.

Wider value

Supporting efforts to retain value and reduce waste.
