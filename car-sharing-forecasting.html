RESEARCH PROJECT / CAR SHARING

Duration and Vehicle Utilization Forecasting for Car Sharing

City-level forecasts of booking duration and vehicle utilisation to support shared-fleet planning.

Data: Co-WheelsMethods: ETS · SARIMA · Linear RegressionForecast horizon: 30 days

01 / OVERVIEW

Project overview

This research examines booking duration and vehicle utilisation in car-sharing services, using real-world operational data from Co-Wheels. It develops a city-level forecasting framework to distinguish planned bookings from realised use and to represent the effects of bookings that cross calendar days.

Automatic and manually specified ETS and SARIMA models, alongside linear regression, produce 30-day forecasts. Their accuracy and robustness are compared across overall and day-of-week evaluation slices.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Research objectives

  • Predict booking duration across cities to inform dynamic fleet allocation.
  • Estimate vehicle utilisation from reservation records and realised usage.
  • Improve forecasting through model selection, time-series decomposition and seasonality analysis.

03 / METHODS

Methodology & evaluation

Data preparation

City-level aggregation, proportional allocation of cross-day activity, temporal segmentation, outlier detection using Z-scores, seasonality-based imputation and structural-breakpoint identification.

Forecasting models

Automatic and manual ETS; automatic and manual SARIMA with weekly seasonal patterns; and linear regression incorporating trend and calendar effects.

Assessment

A 30-day forecast horizon, evaluated using mean absolute error (MAE), mean squared error (MSE) and root mean squared error (RMSE), for overall and day-of-week comparisons.

04 / FINDINGS

Selected findings

The poster's model comparisons show that forecasting performance depends on the evaluation slice. Linear regression achieves the lowest overall MAE, while automatically selected SARIMA achieves the lowest day-of-week MAE. Automatic ETS delivers comparatively balanced performance across both evaluations.
